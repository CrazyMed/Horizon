## [NVIDIA, OpenAI, Microsoft Jointly Release MRC Protocol for AI Supercomputing](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/spectrum-x-ethernet-mrc/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

NVIDIA, OpenAI, and Microsoft jointly released and open-sourced the Multi-path Reliable Connection (MRC) protocol, an RDMA solution leveraging packet spraying technology to enable concurrent traffic transmission across multiple paths with microsecond-level fault re-routing capabilities. This protocol directly addresses GPU idle time caused by network congestion in AI training clusters, potentially unlocking significant efficiency gains for large-scale model training operations. As an OCP open specification, MRC aims to standardize AI networking and accelerate future infrastructure projects like the Stargate initiative. MRC is already deployed on NVIDIA Spectrum-X platform and Blackwell architecture, supporting clusters such as Microsoft Fairwater and Oracle OCI Abilene. The protocol is currently being used for training GPT-5.5 and other large language models, demonstrating real-world applicability at production scale.

telegram · zaihuapd · May 6, 14:39

**Background**: RDMA (Remote Direct Memory Access) enables direct memory-to-memory data transfer without CPU involvement, critical for reducing latency in AI training. Packet spraying is a multipathing technique that distributes packets across equal-cost paths to improve load balancing in data center networks. The OCP (Open Compute Project) is an industry initiative that fosters collaboration among companies like Facebook, Microsoft, and NVIDIA to develop open standards for data center hardware, aiming to reduce fragmentation and development costs.

## [EU Proposes Mandatory Rules to Remove Huawei and ZTE from Telecom Networks](https://t.me/zaihuapd/41247) ⭐️ 6.0/10

The European Commission is developing legally binding regulations that would require all EU member states to completely eliminate Huawei and ZTE equipment from their telecom and broadband infrastructure, replacing the 2020 non-binding "5G security toolbox" recommendations with enforceable rules and penalties for non-compliance. This marks a significant escalation from voluntary guidelines to mandatory requirements, potentially affecting billions in telecom infrastructure investments and signaling a hardening of the EU's position on Chinese technology companies in critical networks. Member states that fail to comply could face EU infringement investigations and financial penalties. The proposed regulations would apply to radio access networks (RAN) and other critical telecom infrastructure, with non-compliant member states subject to investigation procedures similar to other EU treaty violations. The EU also plans to restrict foreign infrastructure funding, blocking loans for projects using Huawei equipment in non-EU countries.

**Background**: The EU's 2020 5G security toolbox was a coordinated approach to address cybersecurity risks in 5G networks, identifying "high-risk vendors" as a key concern. While it recommended restricting such vendors from critical network functions, compliance was voluntary. The new proposal transforms these recommendations into binding obligations under EU law, with enforcement mechanisms and potential fines for member states that do not meet deadlines for removing designated equipment.
